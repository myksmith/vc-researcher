# Neo: Family Office Investor Search Criteria

## Introduction
This document summarises the search criteria for evaluating family offices as direct investors in Neo's $3M seed raise.

We're looking for their desire to write direct early-stage cheques, and does their wealth or portfolio create a strategic angle for Neo.

## What to search for

Prior investments - be sure to look for their portfolio and what those companies do.

## What Makes a Family Office a Strong Fit

**Direct investment behaviour (essential):**
Family offices that invest DIRECTLY in early-stage startups, and not only in real estate or public markets.
Evidence of named portfolio companies with deal dates and disclosed check sizes is strongly preferred over stated intent alone.

**Source of wealth and strategic alignment:**
Families whose wealth originates from, or who have operating ties to, any of the following sectors — our buyer base — are a strategic fit:

- Telecoms and communications infrastructure
- Utilities and energy
- Banking, insurance, and financial services
- Transportation and logistics
- Health, pharma, and medtech
- Industrials and manufacturing

If the family has operating businesses or portfolio companies in these verticals, that is a strong signal.

**Stage and check size:**
Direct seed or early-stage checks of \$500k–\$3M preferred.
Multi-stage family offices with seed-stage experience are fine.
Family offices that only participate in growth rounds (\$10M+) or buyouts are not a fit.

**Sector focus:**
B2B software, devtools, AI/ML, enterprise SaaS, security, or workflow automation — or a broad technology mandate.
Family offices focused exclusively on real estate, consumer goods, or public-market portfolios are not a fit.

**Geographic alignment:**
UK/Europe-based families, or families with active European portfolio exposure, are preferred given Neo is UK/Europe-first.
Cross-border investing capability is a plus; purely US-domestic family offices with no international track record are a lower priority.

**Decision-making clarity:**
Ideally, a named principal, CIO, or family-office manager who has authority to approve direct startup deals.
Multi-committee approval structures are slower but not disqualifying if a named champion exists.

## Disqualifiers (Poor Fit)

- Invests only as LP in VC funds (fund-of-funds only) with no direct startup deals on record
- Portfolio restricted to real estate, public equities, or private equity buyouts
- No disclosed or verifiable direct tech startup investments in the last 5 years
- Entirely opaque structure with no named decision-maker or contact point
- No investment activity of any kind in the last 3 years

## Warm Introduction Pathways

Family offices rely almost exclusively on trusted introductions.
Cold inbound rarely converts.
Research should surface:

- Shared LP relationships (family offices often co-invest with VC funds they back as LPs)
- Operating-company ties that overlap with Neo's network or customer base
- Named advisors, board members, or intermediaries linked to the family office
- Founder network connections (family offices often follow founders they have backed before)

## Conclusion
The ideal family office for Neo combines: direct early-stage tech investing with evidence of real deals, wealth tied to the industries Neo serves (telecoms, utilities, banking, transport, health, industrials), and a named principal who values strategic portfolio fit alongside financial return.
